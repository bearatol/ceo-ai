# Task-relevant professional profiles

Choose by the user's job, not a prestigious title. These are review prompts,
not claims about every member of a profession. Add the person's domain knowledge,
product familiarity, attention, fatigue and motivation separately. Use only
evidence available to that person at the reviewed entry point.

| Lens | Questions relevant to the job | Evidence and limits |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Marketer | Can I identify the audience, offer, distinct value, claim support and useful next action? Is the message consistent with the entry context? | Use visible copy, creative and supplied campaign context. Do not invent audience research, channel results or conversion lift. |
| Sales professional | Can I judge fit, understand the offer and terms, address likely objections, and identify a clear next step? | Use supplied qualification context and visible terms/proof. Do not fabricate prospects, conversations or buying intent. |
| Business operator | Can I assess the promised outcome, effort, cost, dependencies, tradeoffs and accountability needed for this decision? | Use stated terms and supplied operational facts. Missing numbers remain unknown; no invented ROI or financial recommendation. |
| Legal professional | Are parties, obligations, scope, definitions, exceptions and supporting claims findable and internally consistent? | Limit the lens to supplied text and identified jurisdiction/version. Flag issues for qualified review; do not assert enforceability, compliance or case-specific legal advice without appropriate authorized review. |
| Developer | Can I understand the contract, inputs/outputs, errors, examples, integration assumptions and recovery path? | Use public-facing docs and visible product behavior for the persona. Separate static inspection from executed tests; do not infer supported versions or working integration without evidence. |

Use another professional domain when the job calls for it and state the basis.
Expertise in one domain does not establish expertise in another. A familiar
technical term may aid precision; unexplained product-specific jargon can still
block an expert. Present added detail as a tradeoff to validate, not a universal
requirement. Specialist review never grants permission for external actions,
credential access, publication, regulated decisions or changes to the artifact.
