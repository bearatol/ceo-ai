# Editorial checks

Use these as editing questions, not a phrase blacklist or an AI detector. The
success condition is a useful, accurate text a professional would stand behind.

## Meaning before polish

Identify what the reader should understand or be able to do after reading. Can
they find the relevant action, result or decision without decoding a slogan?
Check the facts and logic before adjusting the voice. Give a claim enough
explanation to be useful, but remove another sentence that only announces its
importance. A precise technical limitation can matter more than another benefit.

Keep numbers, names, conditions, attribution and the strength of the evidence.
Do not replace a missing fact with a plausible statistic. An uncertainty such as
"may" or "not yet tested" is information, not clutter. An editor can remove a
repeated disclaimer without removing the actual limitation.

## Professional voice

Prefer ordinary verbs and specific nouns. Professional writing is precise and
considerate; it need not be formal, promotional or cold. Use technical language
when it helps the intended reader, not as a signal of expertise. Do not replace
an established product term with a new synonym halfway through a task.

In Russian, reconsider "в рамках", "осуществлять", "раскрывает потенциал" and
"важно отметить" when they add no meaning. They can be appropriate in an exact
quotation or a context where they convey something specific. In English, check
whether "seamless", "transformative" or "empowers" identifies a real property.
Ask what the reader learns if the adjective disappears.

Natural rhythm follows the explanation. Let a sentence end when its point is
complete; combine fragments that belong to one thought. Do not impose matching
paragraph lengths, a sentence-length formula, forced groups of three, or random
variation. Keep a useful repetition rather than inventing a strained synonym.

Do not insert unsupported opinions or anecdotes. Do not imitate humanity with
slang, typos, artificial chaos, unfinished thoughts or pretend frustration.
"Simpler" means less effort for this reader, not less substance or respect.

## Reconsider patterns, not isolated words

Check whether importance claims have evidence, analysis explains a mechanism,
and an attributed view names its source. Cut empty praise and vague appeals to
experts. Rework repeated "not X but Y" constructions, trailing participial
clauses and tidy three-part slogans when they obscure the point. Keep them when
they express the actual relationship. An automatic summary, optimistic outlook
or future-challenges section needs a purpose and supporting information.

These prompts adapt observations from the Russian Wikipedia essay linked below.
They do not establish authorship. Its encyclopedia conventions do not dictate a
business email, a personal story or product copy. A requested summary, a useful
list or correct punctuation is not an error simply because the essay mentions it.

## Format and finishing checks

- Interface: is the action or state clear, and can the person tell what happens
  next? Keep a short label short, while leaving conditions near the relevant
  choice. Avoid "Everything is ready" if processing is still running. Prefer
  consistent terms to brand slogans. A professional tone must also work in
  loading, empty, success and error states.
- Business: can the recipient tell what is requested, who acts and by when?
  Preserve agreed commitments and qualifications. Do not invent a deadline or
  turn a neutral request into pressure.
- Technical: do the claim, evidence and scope agree? Do not turn a test on a
  sample into a general guarantee. Check a citation supports the nearby claim;
  do not invent a URL, quotation, author, ISBN or DOI to make a text look rigorous.
- Personal: does the story belong to this author? Keep the supplied perspective,
  without adding memories, feelings or life experience.

Use normal keyboard punctuation in newly written prose unless the user or format
calls for another convention. Preserve exact quotes, code, links, variables and
notation. Use emphasis and lists to help retrieval, not to decorate every point.
No automatic closing slogan or call to action is needed. Read the result once
without the prompt: remove anything a reader would not understand on its own.

## Synthetic examples, not user history

The inputs below are fictional facts supplied for these examples. The rewritten
copy does not authorize a claim about any real product or author.

### Interface: export is running; the Exports section shows completed files; no ETA

Overstated: "Everything is under control. Your data is already on its way."
Useful: "Preparing your file. It will appear in Exports when it is ready."

Шаблонно: "Всё под контролем. Ваши данные уже в пути."
По делу: "Готовим файл. Когда он будет готов, он появится в разделе 'Экспорт'."

The useful version describes the state without inventing a completion time or a
notification channel. If the product does not have an Exports section, change
that detail rather than copying this sentence.

### Business: the sender has committed to sending an estimate on 10 July

Ceremonial: "We greatly value your time and are working tirelessly on a solution."
Useful: "We will send the estimate on 10 July. Please confirm who should receive it."

Церемонно: "Мы высоко ценим ваше время и неустанно работаем над решением."
По делу: "Пришлём смету 10 июля. Подскажите, кому её отправить."

### Technical: a patch passed a local test; production has not been checked

Overstated: "The patch ensures reliable operation in all conditions."
Useful: "The patch passed the local test. We have not checked it in production yet."

С преувеличением: "Исправление гарантирует надёжную работу при любых условиях."
По делу: "Локальный тест прошёл. В production исправление пока не проверяли."

### Personal: reports used to be manual; a new CSV tool works, but large files are slow

Too promotional: "Meet a revolutionary tool that unlocks your productivity."
Too thin: "Made a thing. It reads files. Here's the code."

Useful: "I made a small tool for the reports I kept assembling by hand. It reads
CSV files and puts the totals in one place. Large files are still slow, so that's
what I'm working on next."

"Сделал небольшой инструмент для отчётов, которые раньше собирал вручную.
Он читает CSV и сводит итоги в одном месте. На больших файлах пока тормозит,
с этим ещё разбираюсь."

Do not reuse these anecdotes, dates or promises unless the facts hold for the
current author and product. A person can prefer "сделал" in personal posts and
still use "выпустили" in company release notes. Save context with a preference.

## Review exercises

These are acceptance prompts for human or model review, not a completed benchmark.

- Edit an error message with a known recovery action. Pass if the state and
  recovery are clear, the label fits, and no new capability or deadline appears.
- Edit a technical paragraph containing an unverified result and a supplied
  source. Pass if uncertainty and attribution survive; fail if the rewrite
  invents a citation or broadens the result.
- Edit a personal draft with a distinctive, approved voice. Pass if facts and
  voice survive without added life experience or manufactured informality.
- Review a clear paragraph with a list and a long dash. Pass if it stays clear
  under the requested format; fail if the reviewer labels it AI-written or
  rewrites it solely because of a supposed marker.

## Practice sources and limits

This original workflow also draws on `humanizer-ru`, `natural-writing` and
`write-like-me` practices: preserve meaning, cut inflated claims and use authorized
samples. It does not copy their examples or require them at runtime. Suggestions
to invent voice, remove uncertainty or add disorder are not adopted. The default
keyboard-punctuation convention remains a user-overridable preference.
